04 — Index

The question index.

  1. Your brain predicts the sensation of your own movements and turns it down. Without surprise, the tickle response doesn’t fire. Researchers showed that adding a delay of a fraction of a second between your movement and the touch makes it ticklish again.

  2. Partly. The light, itch-like tickle (knismesis) can be self-produced in a dampened form. The laughter-inducing kind (gargalesis) essentially can’t, because your brain sees it coming.

  3. You normally hear yourself through the air and through the bones of your skull, which adds depth. A recording captures only the air-conducted sound, which is close to what everyone else hears.

  4. The final stretch of a wombat’s intestine has alternating stiff and flexible regions. Stiffer regions contract faster and form the corners; flexible regions form the flat sides.

  5. Slightly. Bananas contain potassium-40, a naturally radioactive isotope, at levels that are completely harmless.

  6. It’s an active response from your nervous system that narrows blood vessels under the skin, not water soaking in. Fingers with damaged nerves don’t wrinkle.

  7. Humming needs air to escape through the nose. With the nose blocked and the mouth closed, the airflow stops within seconds.

  8. Cutting an onion releases an irritating gas that reaches your eyes, and they produce tears to flush it away.
